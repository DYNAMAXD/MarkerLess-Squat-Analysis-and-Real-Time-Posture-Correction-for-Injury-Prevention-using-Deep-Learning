A new local website, Pose2Sim Web, replaces the old plan and the old Kinovea page. You attach the videos, it processes them, saves everything, and shows the angles as a graph. This page says exactly what to do.

Step 4 · Look at the angles

  1. Open the run, click the tab Angles. The synced front and side videos (with the skeleton) are on top; the graph is below.
  2. Tick the angles in the table. There is one column for Left (solid line) and one for Right (dashed). Clicking a name ticks both sides.
  3. Move the mouse over the graph (or touch it): a line follows you and a box shows the value of every ticked angle, the frame number, and the side-video time (the time Kinovea shows). Click to jump the videos to that moment. When you play the video, the line moves with it.
  4. Use "From / to (s)" or "Zoom ±5 s around the cursor" to look closer. "Side-view plane" is the angle as a side camera sees it; "full 3D" is the true 3D angle.
GroupAngles
Joint angles (3 points)knee, hip, shoulder, elbow, ankle, left and right, as the inside angle (180° = straight). Plus trunk lean from vertical. These are what Kinovea can measure.
Pipeline anglesthe ones your classifier used: knee flexion, hip angle, hip flexion, knee valgus, ankle dorsiflexion proxy (left and right), trunk flexion, head forward angle, squat depth, lumbar proxy.

Left and Right are the pose model's own labels (the person's left and right). The wrist angle does not exist: the model has no hand point.

Step 5 · Measure in Kinovea

QuestionAnswer
Which videoThe original side video (DJI_…0003_D.MP4 for video 1), not the small skeleton copy.
Where in the videoOnly in the still holds, in the middle of each hold. In a hold you do not move, so a small timing difference cannot change the angle.
How many moments9 per video: 1 standing at the start, 6 squat holds spread through the video, 2 later standing holds. (Fewer than 8 is too few. If you have time, do both videos.)
How many angles per moment4: knee, hip, shoulder, elbow, on the side with the tapes (right). Trunk lean is an optional 5th. That is about 36 numbers per video.
Which timesDo not hunt for them: in the run, open the tab Compare with Kinovea and press Suggest moments. It finds the still holds and puts the 9 Kinovea times in the sheet.
  1. Open the original side video in Kinovea (free from kinovea.org). Jump to the first time from the sheet (the time shows under the video; use the arrow keys to step by one frame).
  2. Pick the Angle tool and click three tapes in order. The middle joint is the corner: knee = hip → knee → ankle; hip = shoulder → hip → knee; shoulder = hip → shoulder → elbow; elbow = shoulder → elbow → wrist. Zoom in to click the centre of each tape. Kinovea writes the angle value on the video.
  3. Sanity check each number: a straight standing leg is about 170–180°, a deep squat knee about 60–100°. If Kinovea shows the outside angle (for example 190° for a leg you expect at 170°), type 360 minus that number.
  4. How to note it: in the sheet, type the 4 angles on the same row as the time. One row = one moment. Format: the time as Kinovea shows it (0:12.50 or in seconds) and the angle numbers in degrees (172.1).
  5. Save in Kinovea: File → Save (a .kva file, your drawn angles) next to the video, named video1_side.kva. The sheet itself is saved by the website automatically.

Kinovea menus: I wrote these steps from memory and could not check them in your version. If a menu is named differently, tell me.

10 min

Step 6 · Get the final error

  1. Stay on the tab Compare with Kinovea. The result appears under the sheet as you type, for each joint.
  2. bias = the constant error (pipeline minus Kinovea, in degrees). 95% low / high = the ± range: about 95 % of the differences fall between them. MAE = the average size of the error. A plot shows each point.
  3. Press Copy result text and paste it into your report notes. Example of how to read it (not your result): "knee bias +2.1°, limits −3.9° to +8.1°" means the pipeline reads 2.1° higher on average and 95 % of the differences are within −3.9° to +8.1°.
  4. The tab also compares the pipeline's own columns (knee_flexion, hip_angle) with Kinovea, which is the number your classifier used.

How to word it: a pilot with one person and about 18 moments, side view only. Kinovea by hand is itself good to about 1–2°. Tape on the skin and the model's joint point are not at the same spot, which is part of the measured error. It does not validate the front-view knee valgus and varus angles.

Where everything is saved

Every run has its own folder inside pose2sim_web\data\. Nothing is overwritten and your original videos are never touched.
