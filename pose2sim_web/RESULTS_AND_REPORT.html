2. What the results mean

  • Knee: the pipeline reads about 5° lower than the manual reference on average. It is nearly exact when standing (within 1°) and 5-11° low in the squat. MAE 4.9°.
  • Hip: larger and less stable error, MAE 9.5°, with a sign change between standing (+5°) and squatting (-12°). Likely reasons (not tested): the hip keypoint of the pose model is a joint centre inside the pelvis, while the hip tape sits on the skin at the side; in a deep squat the soft tissue and pelvis tilt move these two apart.
  • Where the knee offset comes from: the 2D angle measured directly on the side-camera picture is 4° lower than Kinovea, almost the same as the 3D value (4.9° lower). So the 3D reconstruction agrees with the 2D keypoints; the remaining offset is between "pose-model joint position" and "tape on the skin / human clicking". (Interpretation, not proven.)
  • Side-view plane rows: they read +11° high. The reason is most likely my website's projection step (it flattens the 3D skeleton onto a plane estimated from marker movement); it is not part of the dataset pipeline, and the dataset's knee flexion equals 180 minus the 3D knee angle. Treat these rows as a failed alternative, mention them in an appendix only.
Honest disclosure to keep: before measuring, the plan named the side-view plane as the main comparison. After seeing the results, the main comparison was changed to the full-3D angles, because they are the angles the dataset actually used and the plane projection was found to be biased. Write this openly. Do not hide it.

3. How to write it

3.1 Methods paragraph

Validation. Agreement between pipeline joint angles and a manual reference was assessed in a pilot on one participant. A 2-minute trial (standing, squat holds, repetitions) was recorded with two synchronised cameras (1920x1080, 29.97 fps, about 2.9 m apart, about 89 degrees between viewing directions) calibrated with a checkerboard (intrinsic RMS about 0.35 px). 3D joint positions were obtained with Pose2Sim 0.10.49 (RTMPose, HALPE-26 keypoints, triangulation, 6 Hz Butterworth filter). Skin markers (tape) were placed on the right shoulder, hip, knee and ankle. Nine still postures (three standing, six squat frames from four holds) were selected, and the knee angle (hip-knee-ankle) and hip angle (shoulder-hip-knee) were measured manually in Kinovea on the synchronised side video. The corresponding angles were taken from the pipeline at the same frame. Agreement was summarised as bias, standard deviation of the differences, 95% limits of agreement (bias +/- 1.96 SD) and mean absolute error (MAE), difference defined as pipeline minus Kinovea.

3.2 Results paragraph

For the knee, the full-3D pipeline angle was on average 4.9 degrees lower than the manual reference (SD 3.9 degrees; 95% limits of agreement -12.5 to +2.8 degrees; MAE 4.9 degrees; n = 9 postures). Agreement was close when standing (mean difference -0.2 degrees) and lower in the squat (-7.2 degrees). For the hip, the mean difference was -6.3 degrees (SD 8.4; limits -22.8 to +10.2; MAE 9.5 degrees), with a posture-dependent pattern: +4.8 degrees when standing and -11.9 degrees in the squat. The 2D angle measured on the side-camera image differed from Kinovea by -4.0 degrees (knee), similar to the 3D value.

3.4 Limitations paragraph

Limitations. One participant, one session and one camera set-up; nine postures from seven still holds (several frames come from the same hold, so they are not independent), so the limits of agreement are imprecise. Only static postures were checked; accuracy during movement (motion blur, 29.97 fps, 6 Hz filtering) was not assessed. The reference is a single observer's manual 2D measurement in Kinovea and is itself subject to error (no repeat measurement was done); the tape markers do not coincide exactly with the keypoints predicted by the pose model, which can cause a systematic offset, seen here as a posture-dependent difference at the hip. Lens distortion was not taken into account in the triangulation, no limb-swap handling was applied, and the mean reprojection error was 3.8 px (about 8.7 mm). A 2D reference cannot verify the out-of-plane components of a 3D angle. An initial side-view-plane projection of the 3D data produced a bias of about +11 degrees and was therefore not used for the main result. The agreement reported here may not apply to other participants, faster movements or other camera set-ups.

3.6 If the panel asks

  • Why is the hip worse? Hip tape position vs the model's hip joint centre, plus pelvis tilt in deep squat. It is a systematic offset (+5° standing, -12° squat), not random noise. A correction could be tested in future work.
  • Why did you change the main comparison? The plane projection was my own extra step, it had a +11° offset that the 2D check showed was not in the pose detections or the 3D reconstruction; the 3D angle is what the dataset used.
  • Is 5° good enough for your classifier? Compare with the angle differences between correct and incorrect squats in your data (for example depth and forward lean) before answering; quote those numbers.
  • Why only one person? Time-limited pilot; extending to more people with a second observer is the next step.
