3.7 Repeat for honesty 20 min

After a break (and without looking at your first values), measure again knee and hip at 5 of the 7 moments and write them in a notebook or a second spreadsheet. The difference between your own two measurements shows how reliable the "ground truth" is. If your own repeat difference is, say, 2°, then pipeline differences below about 2° cannot be called errors.

5. How to compute and state the error honestly

The numbers (the website does this; here is what they mean)

NumberFormulaWhat it says
Differencepipeline - Kinovea, per momentpositive = pipeline reads larger
Biasmean of the differencesconstant offset (systematic error)
SDstandard deviation of the differencesscatter (random error)
95 % limits of agreementbias ± 1.96 x SDThe error range: about 95 % of future differences should fall inside
MAEmean of |difference|"on average the pipeline is X° away"
Uncertainty of those limitseach limit ± 1.96 x SD x sqrt(3/n) (Bland-Altman). For n = 7 this is about ± 1.3 x SDWith only 7 moments the limits themselves are rough; write this

A good way to report: one table, one row per angle (knee, hip, trunk; side-view plane first, then full 3D), columns n, bias, SD, 95 % limits, MAE; plus your own repeat difference of Kinovea.

Is it correct to say "this is the error range compared with ground truth, valid for our dataset"?

Do not write: "The angles in our dataset have an error of ±X° compared to ground truth."
Write this instead (fill X, A, B, n):
"Agreement between Pose2Sim joint angles and manual Kinovea measurements was assessed in a pilot on one participant (one 2-minute trial with the dataset's camera setup and software settings). Reflective tape was placed on the right shoulder, hip, knee and ankle. Seven static postures (3 standing, 4 squat holds) were measured by one observer in Kinovea (side view). For the knee, pipeline minus Kinovea was X° (bias) with 95 % limits of agreement from A° to B° (SD Y°, MAE Z°, n = 7). Observer repeatability was W°. These results describe the agreement in this setup only and cannot be generalised to other participants, to dynamic movement, or to the full dataset without further validation."

Why it is not a guaranteed dataset error, and what it still gives you

  • One person, one session: body shape, clothing and lighting differ in the other recordings. Camera positions and calibration quality differ per session.
  • Static postures only. During fast movement, errors can be larger (blur, 30 fps, smoothing filter at 6 Hz).
  • Kinovea is a manual 2D measurement, not a laboratory gold standard, and the tape is not exactly where the pipeline's joint is. Call it "reference" or "manual reference measurement"; the word "ground truth" invites criticism.
  • You can say: the same pipeline with the same settings was used for the dataset, so this pilot gives an indication of the order of magnitude of the angle error. For a proper claim: several participants (at least 5-10), more postures, and two observers.

Limitations paragraph (copy and adapt)

Limitations of the validation: single participant and trial; 7 static postures (21 angle pairs); one observer using Kinovea on 2D video, so the reference itself carries a measurement error of about W degrees (repeat measurement); tape positions do not coincide exactly with the pose-estimation keypoints (RTMPose HALPE-26), so a small constant offset is expected; the 3D reconstruction had a mean reprojection error of 3.8 px (about 8.7 mm), lens distortion was not modelled in the triangulation, and the cameras were 2.9 m apart at about 89 degrees; the side-view-plane angles are the like-for-like comparison with the 2D reference, while full-3D angles cannot be verified by a single 2D view.
