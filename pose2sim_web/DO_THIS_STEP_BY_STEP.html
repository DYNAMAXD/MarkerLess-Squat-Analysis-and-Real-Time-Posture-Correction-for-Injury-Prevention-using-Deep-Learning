Do this, step by step

Goal: measure angles by hand in Kinovea at 9 moments, let the website compare them with the computer's angles, save everything, and write the result for ma'am and the paper. Time: about 1 h 30 min.

The big idea (kid version). The computer looked at your two videos and calculated how bent your knee and hip were. Now you measure the same bend by hand with a digital protractor (Kinovea) on your tape stickers. If your number and the computer's number are close, the computer is accurate. The "error" is just how far apart they are.

3Know your tapes 1 min

You see yourself from the right side. The tape stickers are shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle (outer). For this job you need only 4 of them: shoulder, hip, knee, ankle. Elbow and wrist are not used (arms are not part of a squat).

4The three angles, one by one read once

In Kinovea use the Angle tool (toolbar icon with two lines meeting at a corner). You click 3 points. The 2nd click is the corner where the angle is measured. The number appears next to the corner.

If the number looks wrong:
- Knee/hip shows 250° when you expected 110°: Kinovea gave the outside angle. Type 360 minus it (360 - 250 = 110).
- Trunk shows about 175° when you expected about 5°: type 180 minus it.

How to do C (trunk lean) without getting lost

  1. Click 1 on the shoulder tape. Click 2 on the hip tape.
  2. For click 3 you need a point exactly under the hip tape. Easiest way: switch on Kinovea's Grid (drawing toolbar, the square-grid icon). Its vertical lines are exactly "up-down" on the screen. Put click 3 on the same vertical grid line as the hip tape, lower down on the screen.
  3. Second way if no grid: after click 2, move the mouse straight down from the hip tape and click when the point is directly under it (zoom in, it is easy to see).
  4. Check it works: on the standing moment (moment 1) the trunk number must be small, 0 to 5°. If it shows 10° or more while you stand straight, the point is not straight below. Redo it.

Stuck? Skip trunk lean. It is a bonus. Knee and hip are enough for the report. Just leave the trunk column empty.

How to go to a particular time in Kinovea

Kinovea versions differ a little. Try Way 1. If it does not work, use Way 2 (always works). The time you reach does not have to be perfect: you were standing still for seconds, so anywhere within 0.2 s of the time is fine.

First, find the time display. Under the video there is a slider (the timeline). Next to it Kinovea shows the current time (like 0:00:16.02) and sometimes the frame number. This is what you watch while you move.

Way 1: type the time.

  1. Click once on the current-time number under the video (some versions: double-click it).
  2. Type the time, for example 0:16.02 (or 00:00:16.02), press Enter.
  3. If the video jumps there, done. If the box does nothing, use Way 2.

Way 2: slider + arrow keys (always works).

  1. Click on the slider at roughly the right place. The whole video is 1 min 55 s long, so for 0:16 click about 14 % from the left end. For 1:10 click about 61 % from the left.
  2. Read the time display. Then press the Right arrow (one frame forward) or Left arrow (one frame back). One frame is 0.033 s. 30 presses = 1 second.
  3. Stop when the time display shows the wanted time (±0.2 s is fine). Two or three keys per 0.1 s.
  4. Faster: roll the mouse wheel over the video, or hold the arrow key, to run through frames.

Check you are at the right moment. At a standing moment you must see yourself standing straight. At a squat hold you must see yourself low, not moving. If you see yourself moving, you are in the wrong place: go to the time again.

Frame numbers as a backup: if Kinovea shows a frame number, it equals the frame in the table (give or take 1). You can also set the time display to frames in Kinovea's options (Options -> Preferences -> time format). Use whatever works for you.

Do this at each moment:

  1. Go to the time. Check that the posture looks still and right.
  2. Zoom in on the legs (mouse wheel or the zoom control) so you can click the middle of each tape.
  3. Draw A (knee) and B (hip) (and C trunk only if you do it). Write down the numbers.
  4. Do not delete the drawings. They stay on that moment, and Ctrl+S saves them.
Rule to be honest: do not look at the computer's numbers while you measure. Click, read, write. Compare after.

After 2-3 moments, take a Kinovea snapshot: click the camera icon in Kinovea while your angles are drawn, and save the picture to the folder in step 8. You need 3-4 pictures as evidence.

9Repeat a few for honesty 15 min, optional

After a break measure knee and hip again at moments 1, 2, 3, 5, 6. Write them on paper, not on the website. Difference between your two tries = how steady your own hand is. Use it in the limitations (the "ground truth" also has an error).

10What to say to ma'am (simple words) read

"Ma'am, I recorded one squat session with two calibrated cameras and the computer calculated joint angles in 3D. To check them, I put tape markers on my right shoulder, hip, knee and ankle and measured the angles by hand in Kinovea at 9 moments where I stood still. On average the computer's knee angle differed from my hand measurement by [MAE] degrees. About 95 out of 100 differences lie between [low] and [high] degrees. This is a pilot with one person, so it shows the method works and gives the size of the error, but it needs more people before we can say it for everyone."

Be ready for three questions: Why only one person? (pilot, time; planned next: more people). Why Kinovea? (free, standard, manual measurement on tapes, not a lab system). Why are 3D and 2D different? (Kinovea sees one flat side picture, the computer builds 3D from two cameras; so the side-view version is the fair comparison).

Limitations paragraph

Limitations: one participant and one trial; nine static postures (18 knee and hip angle pairs; use your real count), so the limits of agreement are imprecise; a single observer measured manually on 2D video, so the reference itself has an error of about [w] degrees (repeat measurement); the tape markers do not coincide exactly with the pose-estimation keypoints, so a small constant offset is expected; the 3D reconstruction had a mean reprojection error of 3.8 px (about 8.7 mm), lens distortion was not modelled in the triangulation, and the two cameras were 2.9 m apart at about 89 degrees; side-view-plane angles are the like-for-like comparison with the 2D reference, whereas full-3D angles cannot be verified from one 2D view. The agreement reported here may not apply to other participants, faster movement or different camera set-ups.
