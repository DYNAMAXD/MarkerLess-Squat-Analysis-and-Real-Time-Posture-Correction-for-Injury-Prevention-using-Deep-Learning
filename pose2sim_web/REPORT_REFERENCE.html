2. Short answers (copy)

One-line answer

In a one-person pilot, the knee angle was off by about 5 degrees on average and the hip angle by about 10 degrees, compared with manual Kinovea measurements on still postures.

With the error range

Knee: about 5 degrees on average (MAE 4.9), with 95% of differences between -12.5 and +2.8 degrees. Hip: about 10 degrees on average (MAE 9.5), with 95% of differences between -22.8 and +10.2 degrees, and an error that depends on posture (about +5 degrees standing, -12 degrees in the squat). One participant, nine still postures.
Say "on average" every time. Do not say "±5°" or "±10°": that sounds like a promise that the error is never bigger (the knee was off by 11° once).

3. Always attach these three things

  1. "in a pilot with one person"
  2. "on still postures, not during fast movement"
  3. "compared with a manual Kinovea measurement, not a lab system"

5. Facts to disclose (put in Methods or Limitations)

  • Main comparison was changed after seeing the data. Planned main = "side-view plane" angle. It showed a bias of about +11° (my own website's projection step, not part of the dataset pipeline), so the main result is the full-3D angle, which is what the dataset used (knee flexion = 180 minus the 3D knee angle). The plane results go in an appendix.
  • Diagnostic: the 2D angle measured on the side-camera image differed from Kinovea by -4.0° (knee) and -3.5° (hip), similar to the 3D knee value, so the 3D reconstruction is consistent with the 2D keypoints.
  • Hip error depends on posture (+4.8° standing, -11.9° squat); likely tape position vs the model's hip joint centre (not tested).
  • Reprojection error 3.8 px (about 8.7 mm); lens distortion not modelled; no limb-swap handling; cameras 2.9 m apart, about 89°; 29.97 fps; 6 Hz Butterworth filter; intrinsic RMS about 0.35 px.
  • One observer, no repeat measurement (the Kinovea reference error is unknown). Several squat frames come from the same hold (not independent).
  • Do not quote "r = 1.00" (it only reflects the large difference between standing and squat angles).

6. Likely questions

QuestionAnswer
How accurate is it?About 5° (knee) and 10° (hip) on average in this pilot. I report it as agreement with a manual reference.
Does it apply to your whole dataset?Same set-up and software settings, so it indicates the size of the error. It is not proven for every person; more participants are needed.
Why only one person?Time-limited pilot; next step is more people and a second observer.
Why is the hip worse?The hip tape sits on the skin, the model's hip is a joint centre inside the pelvis; the offset changes with posture.
Why did you change the main result?The plane projection was my own extra step with a +11° offset not present in the 2D detections or the 3D reconstruction. The 3D angle is what the dataset used. I disclose the change.
Is 5° good enough for the classifier?Compare with the angle differences between correct and incorrect squats in your data before answering, and quote them. (Not yet checked.)
Is it as good as other systems?Not compared with published studies yet; do a literature check before saying so.
