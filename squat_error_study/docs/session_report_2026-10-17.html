4. The Neck-missing problem: what to do

The Neck is missing for the whole recording in 13 recordings (147 reps, about 12%). In those, the pipeline’s trunk angle is wrong: maximum about 1° instead of about 28°. A model trained on it would see “perfectly upright” trunks, which would hurt forward_lean learning.

5. Why 5-fold cross-validation, split by person

It is the way the models are evaluated, not a model. The 18 persons are split into 5 groups. Each round trains on 4 groups and tests on the 5th. After 5 rounds, every person has been tested exactly once on a model that never saw them.

Why this and not one train/test split

  • Only 18 persons. A single split puts 3–4 persons in the test set; the score would depend on who happened to land there. Five rounds give a mean and a spread.
  • Uses all the data for both training and testing, which matters when data is small.
  • Split by person, not by rep. One person’s reps look alike. If their reps were in both train and test, the model would recognise the person and the score would be inflated.
  • Test persons are never used to pick the best epoch. Two training persons are held out inside each round for that.

Why 5 and not another number

ChoicePersons per test groupCostVerdict
2-fold9cheaptrains on only half the data
5-fold3–45 trainingsstandard compromise
10-foldabout 210 trainingsvery noisy per fold, 2× the time
Leave-one-person-out118 trainingsmost data-efficient but 18× the time; possible for the final model only

5-fold is a convention, not a law. It is the usual balance between enough training data per round and a stable score. With the GPU you can add repeats (--seeds 0 1 2), which re-draws which persons go in which group, and report the mean over all of them.

The final model for the product is then trained once on all persons (--final).
